💴 Money & Payments
💳

IC Card (Suica / Pasmo)

Get at Narita or any major station. Tap on/off for trains, buses, taxis, and 7-Eleven. Top up at station machines — keep ¥2,000+ loaded.

🏧

Cash & ATMs

7-Eleven ATMs reliably accept foreign cards. Carry ¥5,000–10,000 daily — small restaurants and shrines are often cash-only.

🙅

Do NOT Tip

Tipping is rude in Japan. Exceptional service is standard — it is a point of cultural pride, not an expectation of extra payment.

🚇 Getting Around
🚕

Taxi Apps

Use GO or Uber. Taxis start at ~¥750. Clean, safe, and drivers use GPS — you don't need to speak Japanese.

🗺️

Google Maps Transit

Extremely accurate in Tokyo. Download offline Tokyo metro maps before leaving Austin. Note exit numbers — they matter in large stations.

🟢

Yamanote Line (Green)

Loops through Shibuya, Shinjuku, Harajuku, Ueno, Akihabara, Tokyo Station. Runs every 2–3 minutes. Your most-used line.

🔵

Hibiya Line (Silver/Grey)

Runs past Ebisu (your hotel). Connects to Roppongi (3 stops), Ueno (25 min), and Ginza.

🎌 Etiquette
🤫

Quiet in Public

Speak softly on trains. Phone calls on trains are impolite. Eating while walking is frowned upon in many areas.

🗑️

Almost No Public Trash Cans

Carry a small bag for wrappers. Convenience stores have bins — buy a drink or snack if you need to dispose of something.

👟

Remove Shoes at Temples & Traditional Spaces

Look for the raised step (threshold) as your cue. Slip-on shoes are easier. Leave them neatly pointing outward.
