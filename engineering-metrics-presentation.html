The Challenge: Platform in Transition

Strategic Migration

  • Moving from on-premise to SaaS over 3-5 years
  • Currently 40% on-premise, 60% SaaS
  • Migrating 10% of revenue per year
  • Maintaining both platforms during transition

Key Tension

Our largest, most loyal enterprise customers prefer on-premise for security, compliance, and existing infrastructure investments. We must maintain excellence on both platforms while gradually proving SaaS delivers superior ROI.

Bottom line: We need metrics that work for both models and guide our transition.

Why These Metrics Matter

What You Measure Determines What You Improve

✓ Good Metrics

  • Drive the right behaviors
  • Create shared language
  • Enable informed decisions
  • Highlight systemic issues
  • Celebrate progress

✗ Bad Metrics

  • Create perverse incentives
  • Encourage gaming the system
  • Focus on activity vs. outcomes
  • Ignore context
  • Punish transparency

Our Commitment: These metrics are diagnostic tools, not performance evaluations. They help us spot trends, share learnings, and improve together.

1. Reliability

Are we keeping the lights on?

Metric Enterprise Target SMB Target
Uptime % 99.9% (contractual SLA) 99.5% (balance speed/stability)
SLA Attainment 100% (zero tolerance) 95% (rapid recovery OK)
Telemetry Coverage 100% before deployment Instrument iteratively

💡 Key Insight

Different risk profiles require different targets. Enterprise customers have contractual SLAs; SMB customers value speed and can tolerate occasional issues if we recover quickly.

How We Measure

  • Azure Application Insights for uptime monitoring
  • PagerDuty for incident tracking and SLA compliance
  • Quarterly telemetry audits by product

2. Velocity

Are we delivering predictably?

Key Metrics

  • Team Velocity Trends: Track story points and work items per sprint. Target: ±10% variation sprint-to-sprint
  • Deployment Frequency: Context matters—SMB teams deploy daily, enterprise teams deploy bi-annually. Both are "good" if predictable.
  • Release Schedule Integrity (Enterprise): Did we deliver what we committed? Target: 90%+ features per release

⚠️ Common Pitfall

Don't compare velocity across teams. Story points are relative. Instead, track trends within each team and celebrate improvement.

How We Measure

  • Azure DevOps APIs for velocity tracking
  • Sprint retrospectives for context (onboarding, tech debt sprints, etc.)
  • Quarterly release integrity reports
7

3. Quality

Are we building it right?

Key Metrics

  • Change Failure Rate: % of deployments causing production issues. Target: <15% for both models
  • Customer Defect Trends: Track incoming vs. fixed defects by severity (High/Medium/Low)
  • Defect Backlog Health: How many defects are aging >30 days, >90 days?
  • Maintenance SLO Compliance: Are we fixing defects within our commitments?
    • High severity: 48 hours
    • Medium severity: 2 weeks
    • Low severity: backlog prioritization

💡 Key Insight

High deployment frequency (SMB) means higher defect discovery rate—that's good! What matters is resolution speed, not absolute defect count.

4. Security

Are we protecting our customers?

Key Metrics (Same for Both Models)

Secure Score (0-100)

Composite metric covering:

  • Code scanning (SAST/DAST)
  • Dependency vulnerability management
  • Access controls and least privilege
  • Security training compliance

Target: 85+ for all teams

SOC 2 Compliance State

Track open findings by severity. Target: Zero critical/high severity findings

🚨 This is a Gate

If secure score drops below threshold, feature work pauses until resolved. Leadership enforces this.

5. Focus

Are we working on the right things?

Work Breakdown Analysis

Category Target % Definition
Maintenance 40% Keeping existing features running (bug fixes, support)
Tech Debt 30% Reducing complexity, improving systems
Innovation 30% New features, capabilities, customer value

🎯 Real Example

Three teams were spending 65%+ time on maintenance due to an aging shared service. We carved out a dedicated modernization team. Within two quarters, those teams rebalanced to 40/30/30 and velocity increased 25%.

Note: Enterprise teams may skew higher on maintenance due to product maturity—that's expected and acceptable.

6. Platform Migration Progress

Are we moving toward SaaS successfully?

Key Metrics

  • Feature Parity: What % of on-premise features are available in SaaS? Target: 95% parity within 18 months
  • Revenue Migration Rate: Are we hitting our 10% YoY migration target?
  • Customer Migration Readiness: How many customers are technically ready but haven't migrated yet?
  • Migration Blockers: What technical debt or feature gaps are preventing migration?

💡 Why This Matters

Without explicit migration metrics, teams will optimize for their local platform and the transition will stall. This dimension creates accountability for the org-wide strategic goal.

How We Measure

  • Quarterly feature audits (on-prem vs. SaaS capabilities)
  • Customer success team surveys
  • Product backlog analysis for migration-blocking work
11

7. Customer Success by Platform

Are customers getting value?

Key Metrics

Retention & Growth

  • Gross Revenue Retention: Target: 100% (both platforms)
  • Net Revenue Retention: Target: 110%+ (both platforms)
  • Expansion ARR: Track upsells and cross-sells by platform

Satisfaction

  • CSAT Score: Target: 85%+ (both platforms)
  • NPS by Platform: Are SaaS customers promoters?
  • Support Ticket Trends: By platform, by severity

🎯 Strategic Question

If SaaS customers are significantly happier/stickier than on-prem, that's the data we need to justify accelerating migration investments or offering incentives.

Your Team Dashboard Template

What you'll track and report

Weekly: Team Retrospective

  • Review your team's metrics from last sprint
  • Discuss trends—what improved, what declined?
  • Identify 1-2 improvement actions for next sprint
  • This is a team conversation, not a report-out

Monthly: Product Leadership Review

  • Product leads review aggregated metrics across their teams
  • Spot outliers (both positive and negative)
  • Share learnings across teams
  • Escalate systemic issues

Quarterly: Portfolio Review

  • Executive team reviews organization-wide trends
  • Make strategic decisions: rebalance focus, adjust targets, fund tech debt
  • Celebrate wins and course-correct

Implementation: Getting Started

Phase 1: Setup (This Month)

  • Each team identifies their dashboard owner (typically Scrum Master or Tech Lead)
  • Teams configure their Azure DevOps/Application Insights/PagerDuty dashboards
  • First metrics snapshot captured as baseline

Phase 2: Learn (Next 3 Months)

  • Teams review metrics in retrospectives—focus on understanding, not judgment
  • Product leads share patterns across teams
  • We'll iterate on targets and definitions based on feedback

Phase 3: Optimize (Ongoing)

  • Metrics become part of planning: "What will improve this metric?"
  • Executive team uses trends to make strategic investments
  • We celebrate teams that improve and share their practices

Remember: Metrics are a conversation starter, not a scorecard. The goal is transparency and continuous improvement.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Mistake #1: Gaming the System

If metrics are tied directly to performance reviews, teams will optimize for the metric instead of the outcome. Keep metrics diagnostic, not evaluative.

❌ Mistake #2: Ignoring Context

A team's velocity drops 30%—is that bad? Not if they onboarded 3 new engineers and deliberately slowed to invest in training. Always ask "why?"

❌ Mistake #3: Comparing Teams

Don't rank teams by velocity or story points. Context varies wildly. Instead, track each team's trend over time.

❌ Mistake #4: Too Many Metrics

We deliberately keep this to 15-20 metrics total. More than that and teams spend more time reporting than improving.
